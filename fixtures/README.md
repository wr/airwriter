# Fixtures

`checker.urf` / `checker.expected.bin` — synthetic, generated by
`tools/rip/test/make_fixture.py`. The byte-for-byte regression target.
Regenerate the expected stream only when the output is meant to change,
and say why in the commit message.

`ios-*.urf` — captured from a real iPhone through `tools/ippcap/ippcap.py`.
`ios-*.expected.bin` — the printer stream that capture rips to. It is in
`make regress` alongside the synthetic one, and `test/test_ios_capture.c`
decodes the raw capture header for header-field regressions.

`pap-iw701-page.bin` — one short page as Apple's AppleTalk ImageWriter 7.0.1 (Mac OS 8.1, an
SE/30) sent it over PAP to board 1's LocalTalk node, captured with `GET /api/lt/job` (W-1051).
Two prints were byte-identical. It is native ImageWriter II commands, the serial driver's
stream: no `ESC ?`, no PAP EOF (the job ends with CloseConn); `docs/traps.md` has the exchange.
The input for `W-1053 LocalTalk: print PAP jobs from the Mac's AppleTalk ImageWriter driver`.

## What iOS sent

Fill this in from the ippcap log after the first capture. It answers the
question SPEC.md 3.3 leaves open: whether a client honours RS72-144 or
ignores it and sends 300 dpi anyway.

| Capture | Width | Height | dpi | bpp | Colorspace | Duplex |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ios-20260804-232652.urf` | 1224 | 1584 | 144 | 8 | 0 (sGray) | 1 (simplex) |

**iOS honoured `RS72-144`.** It rendered at exactly 144 dpi rather than sending 300
and leaving us to downsample, so the fallback contemplated in `SPEC.md` §3.3 is not
needed and `RS300` does not have to be advertised. 1224 x 1584 at 144 dpi is 8.5 x 11
inches, the full media size including the margins — so the raster always arrives
72 dots wider than the printer's 1152, and the margin crop in `tools/rip/main.c` is
on the normal path, not an edge case.

Source was a Safari page in dark mode, which is why the preview is mostly black.
Useful as a worst-case ribbon and throughput fixture; not representative of a
typical document.
