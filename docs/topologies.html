AirWriter  /  wiring

Your printer stops being a printer.It becomes a network printer.

Once AirWriter is in the middle, everything else — your iPhone, your SE/30 — just talks to it over the network. Here is every way that can be plugged together.

Serial cable
LocalTalk (wired)
Wi-Fi

What you have now

Today

One cable, two devices. The iPhone has no way in.

Add AirPrint, unplug the Mac

Ships first

AirWriter takes the printer's serial port. The iPhone prints. The SE/30 has nowhere to plug in — that port is gone.

You need: nothing extra. This is the first thing that will work.

Keep the Mac, over a LocalTalk cable

The one I'd build toward

The SE/30 moves off serial and onto LocalTalk. It sees AirWriter in the Chooser and prints as normal. The iPhone prints at the same time.

You need: two LocalTalk connector boxes, about $15 on eBay.
One for the SE/30, one for AirWriter. They're what turn two ports into a network.

Keep the Mac, over Wi-Fi instead

Alternative

An AirTalk dongle plugs into the SE/30 and carries LocalTalk over Wi-Fi. No cables between rooms, no connector boxes.

You need: an AirTalk dongle.
The catch: its Wi-Fi format is someone else's invention. If they change it, this breaks.

If the printer is already on LocalTalk

Different printer

Nothing gets displaced. LocalTalk is a network, so the printer, the Mac and AirWriter all sit on it together. The Mac keeps printing exactly as before.

You need: connector boxes for each device, as any LocalTalk setup already has.

The thing worth remembering: a serial cable joins exactly two devices, and LocalTalk joins as many as you like. That single difference decides every layout above — it's why the serial printer forces the Mac to move, and why the LocalTalk printer doesn't.
