# Rev 2026.11 main board and top case — design

`W-1091 Rev B: the main board's next revision`, with
`W-1043 Rev B: through-hole dome LED for the power indicator` under it. Started with Wells in
chat on 2026-10-28. Rev 2026.11 is the name printed on the top case; older docs and Linear call
the same board rev B.

> **Amended 2026-10-28, after routing.** The board as drawn is `docs/hardware-rev-2026-11.md`.
> Where this design and that file differ, the file is right:
>
> - **Who draws the traces.** Wells chose to have Claude draw them along chosen paths, then
>   review. That replaces "Wells draws every new trace" under Constraints. Long runs are drawn
>   point by point, and short stubs round a part's own pins are found by a small grid search
>   (`tools_pcb/r13_reroute.py`, `r13_route.py`). There is still no autorouter. Rev A copper
>   and unanchored parts were rerouted or moved where that made the new runs short and
>   straight. All rev A copper that still applies is kept.
> - **No DIP switches** (Wells, 2026-10-28: "nix the DIPs"). S1 and R28 are gone. The
>   settings page already does what they did: the AppleTalk mode, the screens, a test page;
>   gamer RGB moves to the page. That frees eight GPIOs and the board's west edge, and the
>   tray loses its DIP slot.
> - **J4's handshakes.**
>   - U3's channels are swapped: J2 is on T2/R2 (pins 7–10), J4 on T1/R1 (pins 11–14), so
>     J4's lines leave U3 on the side facing J4.
>   - Each port's HSKo has its own GPIO, on pins the DIPs freed: J2's on IO47 (R24 to GND),
>     J4's on IO13. J4's HSKi reaches IO21 through U3's receiver 1.
>   - J4's HSKo drive has no pull-down (R31 is gone): it would sit at the end of an 8 mm
>     stub, and nothing on J4 reads pin 1 at power-up.
>   - The cable test drives each HSKo; IO8 reads J2.2 and IO21 reads J4.2.
> - **U1 is the ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R8** (Wells, 2026-10-28): 16 MB of flash and 8 MB of
>   octal PSRAM, against rev A's 8 MB and 2 MB, so the firmware has room to grow and a job
>   can buffer whole photo pages. Its PSRAM takes IO35–IO37, which the DIPs' removal freed.
>   It needs its own firmware build (octal PSRAM, a 16 MB partition table), and it is rated
>   to 65 °C, not 85.
> - **SW1 is the BZCN TSB001A3518A**, the Macintosh Mini's side switch (Wells, 2026-10-28:
>   rev A's EVQP44B3M "very easily lifts off the board"). Two plastic pegs and two bracket
>   pads hold it; its button faces the case's pinhole on rev A's axis, 1.90 mm up.
> - **IO3's pull-up (R26) is dropped.** IO3 is boxed in by rev A copper, and it is only read
>   if `EFUSE_STRAP_JTAG_SEL` is burned, which no AirWriter does.
> - **Straps.** The revision strap is IO16 (R32) and the variant strap is IO15 (R33). These
>   are the unused 32 kHz crystal pins, and both resistors sit just north of the module.
> - **VBUS at H4.** The one via is grown to 0.5 mm drill, 0.9 mm pad, in place of a second
>   via. Its plated wall now holds more copper than the 0.5 mm track that feeds it, so that
>   track, unchanged from rev A, is the limit and a second via would add nothing.
> - **J2's receive resistors (R29, R30) are 15 k, not 10 k.** U2's input resistance is 12 k
>   minimum but 15 k typical, so with 10 k a −15 V RS-232 line reaches about −9 V, past the
>   SP3490's −7 V common-mode limit. With 15 k, −12 V lands at −5.3 to −6.0 V and −15 V at
>   −6.7 to −7.5 V; an RS-422 printer's 2 V still arrives as at least 0.89 V against a 0.2 V
>   threshold, and the RC stays near 150 ns.
> - **U5 in line on D−.** J1's D− runs into U5's pad 3 on its way to the ESP32. U5's ground
>   pad gets its own via under U5.
> - **Firmware** is `W-1109 Firmware for rev 2026.11 boards: N16R8 build, straps, own handshake pins, no
>   DIPs, Lite`.

## Why

Rev A is board 1. It prints from phones and Macs, but it is a prototype with bodges and a list
of lessons (`docs/hardware-rev-a.md`, "Rev B candidates"; review #4's rev-B list). Rev 2026.11
is the board AirWriter sells (Wells, 2026-10-28), ordered at PCBWay in November or December
2026. One board makes all three things the shop lists:

- **AirWriter** ($139): the LED array under the gold top case.
- **AirWriter Lite** ($99): the same board without the LED array, under the plain lid.
- **TashTalk add-on** ($19): the PIC and the LocalTalk socket. It is fitted at the bench when
  bought with a board, or by the buyer later.

A board that sells has to:

- start every time it is plugged in;
- survive whatever printer and cable people plug into it;
- be built by the fab with as little hand work as possible.

Rev A misses on all three:

- It sometimes powers up in download mode.
- Its empty chip socket leaves a transceiver enable floating.
- An RS-232 printer's transmit line can exceed its receiver's rating.
- Every board needs eleven hand-soldered joints.

## Goals

1. **Every power-up runs the firmware.** No download-mode boots. Before the firmware runs,
   nothing reaches the printer, the LocalTalk bus or the LED chain.
2. **The printer port is ready for every printer family the firmware is reaching for.**
   - ImageWriters, as today.
   - StyleWriters (`W-1103`): the handshake line out is held asserted.
   - QuickDraw LaserWriters (`W-1102`): HSKi passes a ~1 MHz clock.
   - RS-232 printers both ways: the original ImageWriter and LaserJets (`W-1083`).
3. **Base boards are safe as shipped.** They ship with an empty PIC socket and no LocalTalk
   socket, and a TashTalk add-on fitted later just works.
4. **The power light is plainly visible** through the top case's slash.
5. **PCBWay builds it.** Every SMD part, the printer socket and the power LED are placed by the fab. Full and
   Lite come from one board, with two assembly lists.
6. **The lit dots are crisp** (Wells, 2026-10-28: "kinda fuzzy"), and the test button stands
   about 0.3 mm proud of the top case, not 1.3 mm.
7. **Nothing else changes.** Same case, same top case art, same lattice approach, one firmware
   image for rev A and rev 2026.11.

## Non-goals

These were on the rev B list and are dropped.

- **0 Ω cable-option pads on J2.** Since review #4 the port is wired as a Mac's, so Apple's
  ImageWriter cables are the right cables, and they are the cables every one of these printers
  uses with a Mac. The pads would only serve a straight cable, which nobody should buy.
- **Deleting U3 by scoping HSKo.** U3 is now what carries the StyleWriter handshake, the fast
  clock and J4's handshakes (below).
- **Inrush soft-start.** VBUS bulk is about 4× USB 2.0's 10 µF attach budget. A Mac's port or
  a charger takes it. A bus-powered hub might drop its other devices for a moment. The product
  carries no USB logo, so the limit is advisory. Accepted and written down.
- **An R/C tie on J4's shell.** LocalTalk boxes are transformer-isolated and a Mac's port
  shell is its ground, so a hard tie to GND is right. Documented as deliberate.

Also out of scope:

- J2 pin 7 (GPi): nothing on the bench or in the designs needs it.
- A LED power switch.
- USB-C power delivery.
- Bottom-side assembly.
- A change to the board's size or outline.
- Product labelling and regulatory marks. They belong on the case, box and manual, which are
  easier to change than copper.

## Anti-goals

- **Anything that moves a part the case or top case lines up with**, relative to the case:
  - a lit dot (37 LEDs) or the test plunger;
  - a mounting hole;
  - a port, the USB-C or the antenna;
  - S1's keys or SW1's plate.

  `tools_pcb/r7_verify.py` must still read 37/37 and the plunger at 0.0000 mm.
- **Leaving PCBWay's standard tier.** The drill floor stays 0.30 mm, and track and space stay
  0.15 mm, apart from the existing 0.12 mm exception at U4.
- **A board that needs a different firmware build.**
- **A part only one distributor has, where a stocked one does the job.**
- **An autorouter on this board.** Wells draws the traces himself (2026-10-28). Everything
  else (schematic, parts, placement, the checks) is done and checked before he is handed the
  board, and checked again after he routes.

## Changes

The current schematic and board are the starting point (`hardware/airwriter/`, rev A as
ordered). Rev A stays retrievable from a git tag (`hw-rev-a`) made before the first edit.

### Power-up and quiet lines

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| IO0 (`/BOOT_CFG`): 10 k pull-up to 3V3; C7 removed | C7 against the S3's ~45 k internal pull-up sometimes holds IO0 low past EN, and board 1 boots into download mode (2026-10-28, `GPIO_STRAP` 0x23). Espressif recommends a pull-up on GPIO0 and says not to put large capacitors on it. SW2 still pulls IO0 low for a manual download. |
| `LT_DE`: 10 k to GND (across U6 pins 3–5) | Floats with the PIC absent or in reset. Base boards ship with the socket empty. |
| IO48 / U9 pin 2: 10 k to GND | IO48 floats (no pull) through the ROM and early bootloader, until `bootloader_after_init()` (`airwriter_boot/boot_hooks.c`) drives it low; latched LEDs can collapse VBUS into a brownout loop. 10 k rather than review #4's 100 k: held firmly inside the LED field, and the same part as every other pull here. |
| IO35 (`HSKI_DRV`) / U3 T1IN: 10 k to GND | Floats from power-on until the same bootloader hook drives it low (SP3232 driver inputs have no pulls). A pull-down rather than a hard tie keeps the line the firmware's. |
| IO17 (`UART_TX`): 10 k pull-up to 3V3 | Floats at reset. The pull-up holds the printer's line at idle (mark) through reset and download mode, so a power-up sends the printer no stray bytes. A likely suspect for the "printer sat busy after board 1 lost power" issue. |
| IO3: 10 k pull-**up** to 3V3 | Espressif: no internal pull, and it must not float. It is read only if `EFUSE_STRAP_JTAG_SEL` is ever burned; then 1 keeps JTAG on USB and 0 moves it onto GPIO39–42, the DIP legs. Review #4's pull-down was the wrong way. |
| `LT_RESET`: 1 k in series | Protects IO2 from ~9 V if someone puts a PIC programmer on the socket with the PIC seated. |
| DIP3 (GPIO40): 1 k in series | With USB-OTG download enabled (the eFuse is unburned on every module since date code 2219), the ROM drives GPIO40 high at start-up (TRM Table 33.4-2), and a closed DIP3 shorts it to GND. The ROM drives GPIO38/39 low and leaves 41, 42 and 21 as inputs, so no other leg needs one: review #4's "1 k in every leg" comes down to this one. Burning the eFuse instead is irreversible. |

### Printer port (J2)

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| R9 fitted (0 Ω) | J2 pin 1 (HSKo) asserted at RS-232 levels from power-on, as a Mac's port holds it. A StyleWriter won't answer otherwise (InkTalk). An ImageWriter takes it on DSR and ignores it. |
| U3 → SP3232EBEN-L/TR (LCSC C6852) | Same SOIC-16 and pinout. Receiver delay 0.15 µs and skew 50 ns typ, against the fitted EEN's 0.3 µs and 200 ns typ / 1 µs max, so HSKi can carry the ~1 MHz clock of `W-1102`. Also −40 to 85 °C, ±25 V receiver inputs, and ±15 kV ESD. Chosen over the SP3232EU, which is 0–70 °C only in SOIC-16 and rated ±15 V on its inputs. Both are Active at MaxLinear. All the speed figures are typical, so board 1 takes the swap by hand first and is checked at 1 MHz. |
| 10 k (1 %) in series with each of U2's receiver inputs (J2 pins 5 and 8); R5, R6 and R7 removed | An RS-232 printer's TxD (±12 V, up to ±15 V) lands on the SP3490's B input through Apple's cable. The SP3490's absolute maximum is ±14 V and its common mode −7 to +12 V. The divider against its ≥ 12 k input resistance brings ±15 V to about ±8 V and −12 V to about −6 V. An RS-422 printer's ≥ 2 V differential still arrives as ≥ 1.1 V against a 0.2 V threshold. At ~10 pF the RC is ~100 ns, fine at 1 Mbit/s. This lets RS-232 printers talk back (PJL status, the original ImageWriter's self-ID), with no extra receiver channel and no firmware switch. R5 (termination) means nothing behind 10 k, and R6/R7's 4.7 k bias would hold the receiver at mark; they were DNP and go, which frees room for the new pair. The printer-off-with-cable-attached state stays undefined, as review #4 accepted. |
| U2's driver to RS-232 receivers: no change | It swings 0–3.3 V on TxD−, which RS-232 receivers read (they switch at ~1.5 V). The Mac's port does the same. Confirmed on paper when an RS-232 printer reaches the bench. |

The SP3490 is rated ±2 kV HBM on its bus pins, and the SP3232 ±15 kV. J2's shell meets the
plug's shield before any pin does and goes to GND through R8, so a discharge on plugging lands
there first. No TVS diodes. The 10 k resistors protect the receive pair, and SM712-type parts
(−7 V standoff) would clamp RS-232 anyway.

### LocalTalk port (J4)

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| J4 pin 1 (HSKo) from U3's spare driver T2OUT; T2IN from a free GPIO with 10 k to GND | J4 becomes a full Mac-style port. The cable test drives each handshake leg through a crossover (J4.1 → J2.2, J2.1 → J4.2), so it catches a cable without its handshake wires. That cable leaves an ImageWriter "out of paper" forever, and S-Video-style mini-DIN cables commonly drop those wires. |
| J4 pin 2 (HSKi) into U3's spare receiver R2IN; R2OUT to a free GPIO | The read half of the same test. R2IN is RS-232-rated (±25 V), so plugging a printer into J4 by mistake stays harmless. |
| Rename `LT_A/LT_B/LT_Y/LT_Z` to match their pins | The names are swapped, but U6/U8 polarity is right. Rename only, no rewiring. |

U6 and U8 stay SP3485EN-L (±2 kV HBM). TashTalk's timing and the fail-safe behaviour on a
shorted idle bus are proven on them, and the LocalTalk box in front of them is
transformer-coupled. A THVD1450 (±30 kV HBM) would be a pin-compatible upgrade if one is ever
wanted. It is not taken here.

### Power and USB

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| U9 → SN74LV1T125DCKR (LCSC C75925) | Same SC-70-5 pinout. Runs from 1.6 to 5.5 V, so USB's 4.40 V floor is in range (the AHCT part wants 4.5 V). Its VIH is about 2.0 V at 4.5–5.5 V against the ESP32's 2.64 V minimum high, and its output swings to VCC for the SK6812's 0.7 × VDD. The 74LVC1G125 the review suggested has VIH = 0.7 × VCC (3.15–3.85 V) at those supplies, so a 3.3 V input fails it. |
| A second VBUS via beside the one at the H4 crossing | One 0.4 mm via carries the whole board's supply (1.94 A modelled worst case). |
| U5 in line on D− | The clamp sits on an 8 mm stub today. Route D− through U5's pads as D+ already is. |

### Power light (`W-1043`)

D3 becomes a 3 mm flat-top frosted green THT LED (TOGIALED, LCSC C20613324: InGaN 518 nm, the
same hue as today's D3, 800–1200 mcd at 20 mA, 110°, 3.85 mm tall with its flange, 22 k in
stock) standing on a 4 mm × 8 mm nylon LED spacer (HIWA LED4*8, LCSC C92326), so its face sits
at about 11.85 ± 0.55 mm, about 1.2 mm under the panel. R17 becomes 150 Ω from 3V3: about
3 mA, about a sixth of its 20 mA brightness, already many times rev A's. It stays on 3V3 so it
still means "the regulator is up" (VBUS and 680 Ω would hold the current steadier across parts
but lose that). The 2 × 5 × 7 mm rectangular LED that would match the slash's shape was
weighed and dropped: the only stocked one is GaP, about 5 mcd at 10 mA, with 50 in stock.

A 3 mm face covers about 3 mm of the 5.2 mm slash; at 1.2 mm below it, a 110° flat top lights
the rest. Mocked up on board 1 before ordering: a loose LED of the part above, wired to D3's
pads at the spacer's height under the top case, judged by Wells and the resistor set by eye.

The LED is centred under the slash, which is 0.8 × 5.2 mm, centred at board (159.35, 160.38)
and leaning at −77.5° in the board frame; its footprint is rotated so its leads run along the
slash. The panel's underside is 13.04 mm above the board. The lattice's D3 tube (`enclosure/lattice.py`, Pro build only) is cut
around the LED's envelope plus the window, not the window alone. It already reads D3 from the
board, so it changes with the footprint. The spacer (4.0 mm) and flange (3.8 mm) need a bore of about 4.5 mm, which leaves the tube thin on the west, towards J1's shell, and running to the board edge on the east: a wall of one printed line (0.45 mm) is accepted there, and where J1's envelope leaves no room the tube may stop below J1's height on that side. Nothing lit is near enough for D3's light to bleed into.

PCBWay fits D3 on its spacer (a BOM line with a note: THT, spacer under the body, lens up), on AirWriter and Lite alike. The shop's Lite has a clear top, so the light shows there too.

### Top case: crisp dots

Why the dots are fuzzy. The top case (`hardware/panel/`) has copper only on its back: a flood
with a window 5.79 mm across (the 5.64 mm dot plus 0.15 mm) behind each lit dot. Its front is
black mask over bare FR4 everywhere but the gold art. Light comes in through the back window
and spreads sideways in the 1.5 mm of FR4, which is a diffuser. Black mask passes some light,
so a halo leaks out through the mask around each dot. On the 14 bare-FR4 dots (`P`) the halo
rings the dot. On the 23 teal dots (`B`, `L`), mask covers the dot and its surroundings alike,
so nothing on the front marks the dot's edge at all. This is Wells's diagnosis, and the
geometry agrees.

The fix is a front copper flood under the mask, made the way the back one is
(`emit_board.py`, beside `back_copper_flood`). It runs everywhere on F.Cu, inset 0.55 mm from
the outline, with a window only where light should leave:

- **Teal dots (`B`, `L`):** the window is exactly the dot (5.6364 mm). The copper edge under
  the mask becomes the dot's edge.
- **Bare-FR4 dots (`P`):** the window is the dot plus 0.10 mm. The mask opening stays the dot,
  so mask registration can never uncover a sliver of copper at the dot's rim. Any halo is now
  at most 0.05 mm wide.
- **The power-light slash:** the window is its outline plus 0.05 mm all round, for the same
  reason. The slash gets crisp too.
- **Gold art and gold dots:** the flood runs under and into them. A misregistered mask opening
  now shows copper (gold) rather than FR4, so gold edges sharpen as well.
- **The test dot:** all front copper goes on the one decorative net, `PANEL_AU`, that the test
  pad, annulus and tab already share, so DRC reads one node.

Side effects:

- Copper on both faces balances the panel, which helps it lie flat.
- The black field becomes mask over copper rather than mask over FR4, uniformly. The look
  changes only at the 0.55 mm border round the outline, where the copper stops (the panel's
  copper-to-edge rule, set for the V-score in the combined order). It may read as a faint rim
  in raking light; Wells judges it on the first panels.
- The art in Figma and the dots' positions don't change. The panel is regenerated with
  `generate.sh` and stays byte-deterministic.

### Test button

SW3 goes from the 16 mm XUNPU TS-1002-BR16026 (LCSC C920211) to the 15 mm TS-1002-BR15026 of
the same family (LCSC C920209: same datasheet, footprint, Ø3.5 plunger, 2.6 N and 0.25 ± 0.1 mm
stroke; 980 in stock). Part number: B silver dome, R no column, 150 the height, 26 the force.

The top case's face is 14.64 mm above the board, so the plunger stands 0.36 mm proud instead of
1.36 (Wells measures about 1.3 today). Pressed flush, it travels 0.36 mm against a 0.25 mm
stroke, so it clicks about 0.1 mm before the finger meets the case. At the far ends of the
tolerances (height −0.2, stroke +0.1) it would not click before flush. Guaranteeing it would
take a 15.5 mm part, and neither 15.5 mm part is in stock. So a handful of C920209 is
press-tested in the real case on board 1 before ordering. If one fails, the fallback is
BZCN TSD001A15026A (LCSC C2888561: 15 mm with a tighter ±0.1 height and a 0.2 mm stroke), but
its drawing shows 6.3 mm pin spacing rather than 6.5, so it needs checking against the
footprint first. The footprint, the plunger position and the case's plunger bore don't change.
SW3's 3D model is set to the new height, so the lattice and case read it right.

### Board identity

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| A revision strap: a free GPIO to GND through 10 k, fitted on every 2026.11 board | Firmware reads it once at boot with the internal pull-up. Rev A floats high. One image serves both revisions and knows whether J4's handshakes and the new port behaviour exist. |
| A variant strap: another free GPIO, 10 k to GND, DNP on AirWriter, fitted on Lite | The firmware knows there is no LED array: no panel mark on the settings page, no panel effects. An erase or reflash can't lose it the way an NVS setting could. |

Free pins on the N8R2, all inputs with no pull at reset: GPIO1, 7, 9–12, 15, 16, 36, 37.
GPIO1–17 glitch low for ~60 µs at power-up (harmless for straps and handshakes); 15/16 are the
unused 32 kHz crystal pins; 36/37 are free only because the PSRAM is quad. Prefer 7 and 9–12;
avoid GPIO1 (a download-mode strap role) and GPIO45/46 (straps).

### Silk, test points, assembly

- **Port names:** SERIAL, LOCALTALK, USB-C and PWR move to the back silk, beside each port
  (the back is a ground plane with room to spare). The front legends that print under the
  connector bodies are dropped. Inside the case neither side shows, so silk is for assembly
  and repair.
- **Identity on the back silk:** "AirWriter rev 2026.11", the source URL
  (github.com/wr/airwriter), and "CERN-OHL-S-2.0", as on the Macintosh Mini breakout.
- **SW1:** gets a front-silk mark.
- **Test pad:** a GND pad on B.Cu beside TP7/TP8, so a probe has ground within reach of TXD0,
  RXD0 and EN.
- **J2 assembled by PCBWay:** the Kycon KMDGX-8S-BS is sourced from Mouser, so J2 goes in the
  position file. This removes the eleven hand joints from every board. That takes three edits:
  clear the exclude-from-position attribute on J2's footprint, change J2's note in
  `make_fab.sh`, and update `docs/ORDER-NOTES.md` item 2. J4 and the PIC stay out: they are the
  TashTalk add-on, and Wells fits them. (My call. Wells can still keep J2 by hand at order
  time.)
- **Lite list:** `make_fab.sh` writes a second BOM and position file, filtered from the
  AirWriter pair by a Lite list in the script (DNP flags live on the board's footprints, so
  `--exclude-dnp` alone can't make it). It leaves out D10–D46, their caps C30–C66, U9 and its
  bypass C29, R20 and the LED bulk cap C21, and adds the variant strap. One package carries
  both pairs; how many of each, and whether as one PCBWay order or two, is decided at order
  time.

## Constraints

- **Outline, holes and fixed positions:** outline, hole positions, the 37 LED positions, the
  plunger, S1, SW1, SW3, J1, J2 and J4 do not move (see Anti-goals).
- **Antenna keepout:** x 156–162, y 127.6–154.5, both layers, stays empty.
- **Fab rules:** two layers, 1.6 mm, 1 oz, ENIG, matte black. Single-sided SMT. Power net
  class 0.5 mm.
- **Combined panel:** the main board and the art panel (unchanged) still order as one
  snap-apart panel (`hardware/combined/`). A Lite-only order can use the same panel and keep
  the art panels.
- **Tooling:**
  - The schematic changes only through konnect (no text edits).
  - Parts and placement change through pcbnew scripts under `tools_pcb/` (re-runnable, one
    job each), with KiCad closed.
  - **Wells draws every new trace in KiCad.** Before he gets the board, it has been updated
    from the schematic, the new parts are placed, and every rev A track the changes make wrong
    is removed: D− to U5, the J2 receive legs where the 10 k resistors go in, R5–R7's, C7's,
    and D3's. Every rev A track that still applies is left alone, and the ratsnest shows
    exactly what is left to draw. He may rip up and redraw anything he likes. No autorouter.
  - Passives without an anchor may move if the strip below y ≈ 127 runs out of room. That
    means R5–R17, the ICs' bypass caps, U9 and its cap. It never means an LED's cap: the
    lattice is cut to the LED caps at 2.87 mm north. A new part sits clear of every lit dot's
    light path, so `r7_verify.py` still passes.
  - `tools_pcb/r6_lib.py`, `r7_verify.py` and `place_leds.py` hard-code the main checkout's
    paths. They are repointed to run from their own location before anything else, or they
    check the wrong board.
  - Recreate the konnect library symlinks first (`AGENTS.md`, Toolchain).

## Firmware that goes with it

A separate Linear issue, done before the boards arrive:

- Read both straps at boot and show the board in `/api/status`.
- Drive T2IN and read R2OUT in the cable test.
- Hide the panel's UI on Lite.

The IO48/IO35/IO4 clamps the bootloader hook and `app_main()` apply today stay as they are; the hook can't be updated over the air, and the new resistors only cover the window before it. With R9 fitted the IO35 clamp holds HSKo asserted. On a base board with no PIC, IO5 and IO6 float; the firmware gives them internal pull-downs when U7 doesn't answer (no resistors).
